# Deep Research Results - LinkedIn Recruiter Mechanics and Market Signals

Updated: 2026-08-27

## How recruiter discovery works

LinkedIn Recruiter uses both explicit skills and implicit skills extracted from the headline, About, job titles, position descriptions, certificates and other profile sections. A skill therefore needs both a standardized Skills entry and credible use inside Experience. Source: [LinkedIn Recruiter Help - Skills filter](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a593591).

Location is part of Skills Match, with a specific exception for the European Economic Area. This means profile copy cannot solve every authorization or geography filter. It can improve relevance after discovery, but it cannot make US-only or Canada-only eligibility disappear.

The introduction section is the first part a profile visitor sees and includes headline, current position, location and contact information. Source: [LinkedIn Help - Introduction section](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a563106/information-displayed-on-the-introduction-section-on-your-profile?lang=en).

LinkedIn recommends using About for mission, motivation and skills and keeping it to one or two paragraphs. Source: [LinkedIn Help - Create a good profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a554351/how-do-i-create-a-good-linkedin-profile-?lang=en).

Featured can hold portfolio links, posts, documents and media as proof, but Featured content itself is not discoverable through search. It is a conversion layer after a recruiter opens the profile, not a search layer. Sources: [LinkedIn Help - Featured FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a552452/featured-section-on-your-profile-faqs) and [Manage featured work samples](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550399).

## Recruiter scan model

The profile should be designed for four consecutive decisions:

1. **Search match:** Does the profile contain the role, domain and skills used in the job description?
2. **Ten-second scan:** Do headline, location, current/last role and first About lines create a coherent candidate?
3. **Credibility check:** Do Experience entries show scale, ownership, collaboration and honest outcomes?
4. **Proof click:** Does Featured lead directly to relevant case studies rather than a generic home page only?

## Repeated 2026 market signals

Across the sampled roles and profiles, the recurring signals are:

- 3+ years is a common mid-level threshold.
- B2B SaaS, enterprise tools and complex workflows form a recognizable specialization.
- Data-heavy UI, tables, filters, bulk actions and permissioned/multi-role workflows are valuable sub-signals.
- Design systems are expected as applied product infrastructure: components, patterns, tokens, documentation and implementation quality.
- End-to-end means more than screens: problem framing, research, flows, prototyping, trade-offs, handoff and QA.
- Code fluency and runnable prototypes are increasingly useful differentiators, especially in AI-forward companies.
- AI is credible when tied to a concrete workflow; generic `AI-powered designer` language is weak.
- A strong online portfolio remains a hard requirement in many roles.

## Risks to control

- Mixing the DSSL shipped redesign with the later public reconstruction.
- Making ongoing self-directed work sound like an active paid contract after spring 2026.
- Presenting prototype QA counts as user or business outcomes.
- Using headline space for Webflow, AI tooling, relocation or a tool list.
- Overloading About with process philosophy already explained on the portfolio site.
- Adding every skill up to LinkedIn's 100-skill limit instead of a defensible, prioritized set.
- Treating Featured as SEO; it supports proof only after profile entry.
