# UX patterns — case-cover scan

## Market standard

1. A single focal object occupies roughly half to two-thirds of the frame.
2. The project name or product category is the largest text.
3. A case-specific color or image creates differentiation between portfolio tiles.
4. Fine details are decorative; the cover is consumed first as a thumbnail.

## Points of divergence

- Proof-first: a large real screen, sometimes inside a device.
- Story-first: an editorial claim with supporting metadata.
- Domain-first: photography or props communicate healthcare, finance or commerce.
- Spectacle-first: perspective stacks, glossy 3D and neon lighting.

## Recommendation

Use a proof-first/editorial hybrid:

- one real screen, front-facing and unobstructed;
- large project name plus a one-line product problem;
- compact metadata: role/scope and evidence level;
- generated background only as a case-specific material metaphor;
- one composition grid shared by all covers;
- never ask Krea to recreate, transform or typeset the interface.

The background may carry depth, texture and domain cues. The screenshot and typography must be composited deterministically after generation.
