# Raw research — product-design case covers

Engagement numbers are Behance signals, not evidence of hiring performance. The eight references were selected for pattern coverage and domain relevance.

| Reference | What the cover does | Background | Information | Useful lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [InsightFi SaaS dashboard](https://www.behance.net/gallery/229384847/SAAS-Dashboard-UIUX-Case-Study) | Text-first editorial slide with two tiny tilted UI cards | White grid | Logo, benefit headline, explanatory copy, three benefits | Strong message hierarchy; too much copy and too little product proof for a thumbnail |
| [B2B tender platform](https://www.behance.net/gallery/245566741/UXUI-of-a-B2B-SaaS-Tender-Supplier-Analysis-Web-App) | Several UI fragments stacked in deep perspective | Dark blue technical texture, oversized title | Product category only | High immediate drama; UI geometry, legibility and credibility are sacrificed |
| [AI automation builder](https://www.behance.net/gallery/239196805/Ai-Agent-Automation-Builder-SAAS-UXUI) | One large workflow screen on a monitor | Black stage with grid planes | Small logo only | One dominant product artifact is memorable; the hardware mockup makes the work feel like advertising rather than a case |
| [Mediso hospital dashboard](https://www.behance.net/gallery/228501339/Mediso-Hospital-Management-Dashboard) | Dashboard in front of a stock-photo medical team | Pale mint studio background | Brand only | Domain becomes obvious instantly; people overpower the interface and can look generic or invented |
| [Docuverse healthcare](https://www.behance.net/gallery/247397693/Docuverse-AI-Healthcare-App-UXUI-Case-Study) | Editorial statement with no product screen | Soft gray field | Problem statement, industry, category, location, year, short description | Excellent context and confidence; weak proof of UI craft in isolation |
| [Amber Engine design system](https://www.behance.net/gallery/164632881/Design-System-for-a-SaaS-B2B-startup) | Layered design-system pages in and around a monitor | Neutral gray studio | “Design System” on the artifact | Shows a system instead of one screen; still uses decorative perspective and device staging |
| [Taletree mobile app](https://www.behance.net/gallery/227291041/Taletree-Kids-Content-App-%28UXUI-Case-Study%29) | One phone, large and central | Pale geometric 3D stage | Product UI only | Clear device category and focal point; background is attractive but interchangeable |
| [Colory mobile case](https://www.behance.net/gallery/173749155/Colory-Mobile-App-UXUI-Case-Study) | Device silhouette plus oversized title | Grainy monochrome photo/texture | Project name, type, year, author, one-line purpose | Best thumbnail hierarchy in the set; title partly covers the artifact and the device screen does not prove the actual product |

## Repeated patterns

- One dominant artifact works better than three equally weighted screenshots.
- Successful covers pair product proof with either a clear title or an unmistakable domain cue.
- Backgrounds usually do one of four jobs: brand color, industry context, spatial stage, or editorial texture.
- Perspective, glossy devices and stacked glass panels create clicks but weaken exact UI inspection.
- Most references omit the designer’s role, validation level and outcome—the information a recruiter needs most.
