# 04 · Homepage architecture, reading order and editorial copy

## Job to be done: hiring manager / Design Lead / international B2B client

1. In 5 seconds: distinguish an actual product designer/design engineer from a generic landing-page artist.
2. In 15 seconds: identify categories and understand one real user decision.
3. In 30–60 seconds: pick a specific case, see real UI and inspect what work was owned; know if it shipped, was accepted as a prototype or is concept work.
4. When ready: reach CV and email or LinkedIn with working, accessible actions.

## Case-cover storyboards: four editable, one fixed

**01 Agent Ops — Evidence at the human checkpoint.** Large native UI event/callout (financial credit + what customer reads), short reason on left, one verified status line. Prefer native desktop screenshot and mobile counterpart. Avoid 3 small dark panes and repeat of the exact same screenshot in a separate hero.

**02 Partner Portal — Ambiguity resolved by a buyer.** Show one source-row case, two real matched alternatives and choice/confirmation. 41/48 remains context/synthetic demo state, **not post-launch impact**. A leading small comparison note can help, but native UI should take ~70% of stage.

**03 TRASSIR Learn — Two tasks; one content system.** Distinct but equal conceptual paths: read an answer now and complete a program. Show one dominant task-led Home and one focused second view if necessary, not three equal-size microscopic documents.

**04 Vet Clinic — Clinical trace, not generic dashboard.** Show the actual visit/patient/medication decision and supporting note where it is clinically safe and approved; screen selection is conditional on currently accepted product artifacts. Until proven, improve spacing and CTA only, not product screenshot claims.

**05 Pawly — unchanged.** Keep exact rendered media and copy, unchanged link, no new cover assets, no cards added before/after to force a different role. Regression image required even though not targeted.

## Product design versus developer proof

'Selected work' must lead with real product decisions. Keep Webflow projects small but thoughtfully composed; they demonstrate implementation skills rather than pretending to be in-depth B2B case studies. Resist a second giant works section. 'How I work' should be clear at scan speed and link to actual evidence rather than invented research phases.

## Content scan acceptance

An unfamiliar visitor should be able to point to (a) designer identity, (b) at least one true product screen, (c) the four distinguishable B2B/product stories plus existing Pawly, (d) which roles/deliverables are real, and (e) contact, after a quick skim. Avoid any outcome text that requires unsupported business analytics.
