01 · Problem

Buyers arrived with a specification. The portal greeted them like a shop.

Integrators bring a ten-to-fifty-line spreadsheet for a dated project. The old dashboard led with a training banner and promotions, and every ambiguous line ended in a message to a manager.

Before · the shipped original opened with a banner and a bonus offer
After · the workspace opens on the purchase in progress and what blocks it

02 · System

Two ways in. One specification. Every line keeps its source.

An XLS import and pasted SKUs produce the same document. File, row, original text and quantity travel with each line all the way to the order.

03 · The human decision

The system narrows the options. The buyer makes the call.

“nvr 8ch poe” matches two recorders that differ in one thing: object detection. The panel compares exactly that, keeps the file’s wording in view, and applies nothing until the buyer confirms.

Compare · only the attribute that separates the candidates
Confirmed · still marked as the buyer’s choice, and reversible

Why not automate it

Compatibility rules for this equipment were not formalised anywhere I could verify. An automatic match would move the risk to the installation site.

04 · Verification

Identity, price and stock answer different questions.

Product Details puts the technical identity beside the buyer’s own price and warehouse stock, and says where each answer comes from.

Product Details · reached from the catalog or from a specification line
Identity
SKU, manufacturer and the specs that separate near-identical cameras
Price
The contract price from ERP, marked Verified with the time it was checked
Stock
Units per warehouse and dispatch time; delivery is priced later

05 · Commercial review

Uncertainty must never look like a price.

A price that moved after import waits on its own line for the buyer’s approval. A missing price stays “Not confirmed”, leaves the total and blocks fulfillment. It never becomes zero.

An unpriced line says so, and offers a way forward
The total names what it counts — and what still blocks the next step

06 · Fulfillment

Same goods, three plans. The buyer chooses the trade-off.

Only after commercial review does the portal compare completion date, handovers and delivery cost. Speed versus fewer shipments becomes a visible choice, not a hidden default.

Fastest by 01 Nov for ₽7,400, or one consolidated handover three days later for ₽4,200

Outcome

A traceable path from spreadsheet to order.

The commercial redesign shipped at DSSL. What you see here is my later rebuild on synthetic data: it demonstrates the decisions, not measured post-launch results.

What it cost

Matching is not compatibility
Ambiguous lines wait for a person. It is the slowest step in the flow, by design.
Unknown is not zero
Unpriced lines stay out of the total until a manager quotes them.
Delivery is priced last
Its cost appears only once a fulfillment plan is chosen.
