01 · The problem

The agent resolved conversations. It also made promises nobody could see.

The client handled subscription billing for around 40,000 business customers. The vendor’s dashboard showed successful resolutions and satisfied customers. Then fourteen refunds promised under an expired promotion surfaced, roughly nineteen days later. The company paid $23,000. The missing measure was the consequence of an apparently successful conversation.

02 · The operating model

Review the commitments, not every message.

A team of 24, spread across three time zones, could not read every automated conversation. I made the commitment the unit of review: a refund, a credit, a plan change or a consequential promise. Repeated causes become clusters, so a reviewer can fix one source instead of repeating the same investigation fourteen times.

03 · Detect and investigate

Find the costly pattern. Verify the individual decision.

The queue ranks open financial exposure and separates common-cause clusters from individual runs. Opening a run brings the written promise, its sources, billing calls and missing checks into one workspace with the verdict. A polished answer is no longer enough: the reviewer can inspect what supports it and what fails.

04 · The human checkpoint

A financial action waits for an accountable person.

The request exposes the amount, policy, billing method, history and time remaining before the decision. The credit and the customer-visible message sit together. Here, $120 becomes a credit on the next invoice; no cash leaves the account. The action opens a final check, and rejection requires a reason that can be routed to the source.

Trade-off · human intervention deliberately reduces automation at the boundary of financial consequences.

05 · Fix the source

Correct the rule. Verify what the agent actually runs.

The policy draft preserves the originating cluster, region, capability, limit and approval authority. The version comparison then separates approval, application and confirmation by the agent. In this fixture, v5 is approved and applied, but the agent still confirms v4. That mismatch stays visible; it cannot be presented as a successful rollout.

06 · Earn autonomy

More autonomy needs evidence. A serious defect takes it back.

Quality makes policy-compliant autonomy the operating measure and opens the runs behind each signal. The ladder grants permissions by capability, with evidence thresholds and a visible next boundary. Readiness is not permission. A severity-1 defect automatically lowers the affected capability; clean evidence and an explicit human decision are needed before it can rise again.

07 · The outcome

Tested with users. Reworked. Accepted by the client.

I delivered the interactive prototype after research with the client and current and future users, then tested and revised it. The client accepted the work and took implementation forward independently. The outcome evidenced here is the tested design and working prototype.

What testing changed
Reviewers needed to see a cluster’s members and exclude false matches before trusting a grouped correction. Testing also led to a short undo window for an incorrect verdict.
The deliberate cost
A person interrupts automation where money or policy is at stake. Missing evidence and unconfirmed changes remain visible until someone resolves them.
My scope
Research, requirements, information architecture, product UX/UI, design system, interactive React / Storybook prototype and testing.
